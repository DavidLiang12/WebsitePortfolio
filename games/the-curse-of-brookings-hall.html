About the Game

Under Washington University’s iconic Brookings Hall, a laboratory holds clues to a forgotten past. Players explore its rooms, interpret written hints, and solve interactive physical puzzles to unravel what happened.

Eerie color filters and a decayed environment keep the exploration tense. The atmosphere and puzzles work together to make each discovery feel uncertain.

02

Design Goals

I wanted atmosphere and information to reinforce each other: a clue draws the player forward, a puzzle rewards their attention, and the next room raises new questions.

Before building in Unity, I sketched room layouts, clue placements, and interaction flows on paper. Mapping the route in advance helped me guide players toward the ending through the environment.

03

Technical Goals

The enemy uses a Mixamo-rigged character and a NavMeshAgent to move between random patrol points. It pauses for randomized intervals before continuing. A forward raycast from its chest detects the player within a set distance, interrupting patrol and switching the animation to a sprint as the chase begins.

When the enemy reaches the player, the game switches to a high-priority Cinemachine camera and triggers the jumpscare animation, flashing light, and audio. After a short delay, the scene transitions to a death screen.

Coroutines control the idle and chase timing. Speed, detection distance, and timing settings are exposed in the Inspector so the patrol and chase behavior can be tuned.

04

Challenges

The biggest challenge was communication across the team. Working on a larger project over several months taught me the importance of regular progress checks and clear communication.

Lighting was another learning process. I experimented with baked and real-time lighting, balancing the time needed to iterate on baked results against the performance cost of real-time lights.
