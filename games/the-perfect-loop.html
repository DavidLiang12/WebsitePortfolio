About the Game

The Perfect LOOP is a minimalist puzzle game about building a single continuous path, based on the theme “Loop.” Each tile gives a clue about how many of its edges belong to the loop. Players place and adjust pieces until everything connects, guided by environmental clues, ambient sound, and visual feedback.

02

Design Goals

I wanted to explore the theme of “loop” through puzzles. My starting inspiration was a river flowing through land and bringing life to its surroundings: more trees needed more water. This became a mechanic in which different tiles require a specific number of surrounding loop edges.

The idea expanded into cities and Mars colonies, giving players a sense of progression through different environments. I sketched a 3 × 3 grid to introduce the mechanic, then added complexity by combining clue types and increasing the grid size. Each level needed testing to ensure it had one unique solution.

Within the jam deadline, I couldn't implement a fully dynamic grid checker. Instead, I separated vertical and horizontal lines into two grid maps and manually defined each level's solution through coordinates. This was less scalable, but let me validate solutions reliably and finish the prototype on time.

03

Challenges

The biggest challenge was the tutorial. I initially hoped the game could teach itself entirely through visuals and discovery, but playtesting showed that the opening was too difficult and the mechanics needed a more gradual introduction.

During the official GMTK livestream, a bug also revealed that the first level allowed multiple solutions. I addressed these problems in a later update. The experience showed me how valuable player feedback is: seeing the game from someone else's perspective helped me improve both its clarity and its level design.
