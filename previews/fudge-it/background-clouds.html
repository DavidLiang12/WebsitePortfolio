Fudge It!

Build a candy factory together. Then outbid, outvote, and outsmart each other. A board game about shared power and very different secret plans.

01

One factory. Four agendas.

Our goal was to make players feel like scheming partners: cooperating to grow one shared engine while competing to benefit from it.

02

Keep what works. Rebuild the rest.

The first prototype borrowed roles, stats, and dice checks from D&D. Choosing rooms created conversation; resolving them felt like a grind. Rather than polish that system, we kept voting and changed what players were voting for.

03

From the screen to the table.

Moving into physical form exposed work that the digital table had hidden: finding starting cards, arranging components, and understanding when to act. One test took about 15 minutes just to set up and explain.

We kept discussion before room voting because negotiation was central to the experience. The changes focused on clearer choices and instructions, rather than removing the interaction that made the game interesting.

04

Give every card a clear purpose.

The colorful visual design separates rooms, objectives, and actions. Each family supports a different decision: what to build, what to collect, or when to interfere.

05

What the playtests showed.

Players rated the dramatic elements and engagement higher in v2, but rules were not easier to understand. That distinction mattered: stronger player interaction did not remove the need for clearer instructions.

The final playtest reported about nine minutes to learn and 30 minutes to finish. Players wanted more time to build combinations, making pacing, room variety, and objective balance the next priorities.
